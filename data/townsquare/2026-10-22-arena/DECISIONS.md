# Decisions — Winterlichtplatz

D-001 · **One generator, three consumers.** All geometry comes from
`tools/townspec.py` via `build/townspec.json`. The place emitter, the renderer and
the validators each read that file. Rejected alternative: hand-authoring the town in
Studio (impossible here) or letting the renderer hold its own copy of the buildings
(would let screenshots drift from the shipped file, which is the one thing this
deposit must not do).

D-002 · **Rojo for the place file.** Rojo 7.5.1 compiles `.rbxmx`/`.luau` into a real
binary `.rbxl`. Verified by round-trip before writing 3,000 parts, which immediately
caught three serialisation traps (see FAILED_ATTEMPTS F-001).

D-003 · **Ten buildings on a ring, not a grid.** A square with a double ring of
streets and four radial streets reads as "town square" from every camera, including
the eye-level and orthographic views. Building angles are fixed at start bearings so
the ring never collides with itself.

D-004 · **35 floors rather than exactly 3 or 4 per building uniformly.** The brief says
"3-4 floors each"; four buildings carry 4 floors and six carry 3, which satisfies the
brief as a range and keeps the roofline varied. Declared here because a reader could
otherwise think it should have been 40.

D-005 · **Residents are anchored block rigs, not Humanoids.** 64 Humanoids with
pathfinding would dominate the frame budget of a square this size and would need
PathfindingService to look right. Anchored parts with a pose function replicate
predictably and cost nothing per frame. The trade is that they cannot be pushed, and
they do not walk up stairs (see README "What this is not").

D-006 · **64 residents, not 60.** The brief said "around 60 humans". 64 was chosen
because it is 6.4 per building, which divides evenly into the group-per-building room
allocation and lets every resident be named, given a role and given a bed in a real
room. All 64 appear in the day renders; at night they are placed at their beds.

D-007 · **CC0 photogrammetry scans, and only real scans.** 12 ambientCG assets, all
`creationMethod == PBRPhotogrammetry`. The first API query returned mostly
`PBRProcedural` assets; those were filtered out because calling a procedural texture a
"scan" would be false. Receipts, including zip and image SHA-256s, are in
`assets/scans/SCAN_RECEIPTS.json`.

D-008 · **Scan colour in the place, scan texture in the renders.** A `.rbxl` cannot
carry uploaded images, so each material's measured mean albedo becomes the part colour
and the nearest engine Material is used. The renderer additionally tiles the real
albedo map, tinted so the surface mean equals the place colour — the two agree on
colour instead of drifting.

D-009 · **Numpy rasteriser instead of Blender.** Blender is not installed on this
worker (2 vCPU, 1.9 GiB, no GPU, no Cycles), unlike earlier crew boxes. The renderer
is honest about being a z-buffer: shadows and point lights yes, global illumination no.

D-010 · **Two independent render processes.** 12 views at 1600×900 do not fit in one
bash timeout; two background processes over disjoint view sets finished them without a
watchdog. Both logs are kept in `evidence/`.

D-011 · **Dollhouse cutaways rather than floor-section drawings.** Two views remove only
the walls, windows, roof and structure of one building (B04 Salt House and B01 Gilded
Lark), leaving slabs, partitions, stairs and furniture. They are generated by the same
code path as every other view, so each cutaway is also a parity proof that those
interiors exist in the place.

D-013 · **A cutaway replaced the upper-floor interior view.** The first-pass upper
interior looked at a blank partition in a light-plaster room and told the reader
nothing. A second cutaway of a different, taller building shows the same thing better
and is honest about the geometry rather than depending on where a camera happens to
land.

D-012 · **Declared accent and planting palettes.** Validator V12 proves every part
colour is either a scan albedo, a scan albedo times one brightness factor, or a colour
declared in the spec under `accents`. Glazing, metalwork, lamp glass and planting are
declared rather than hidden, so "all colours come from scans" cannot be claimed falsely.
